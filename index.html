AI-powered automatic block planning to maximise asset availability for train operations on Indian Railways.

AI-1 reads a submitted maintenance report and keeps a finding only when the source states it. Ready records enter the backlog. Formula 1 ranks them, Formula 2 packs nearby jobs into one possession, and CP-SAT places that possession inside a legal window while minimising chaos score Z.

AI-1 · Report understandingBEFORE THE PLANNER
BDMS submits the report. AI-1 reads it. It does not score urgency, and it does not choose a possession window.
  • Kept — a field stays only when the source sentence states it. The word “critical” is not a severity.
  • Types — rail fracture, weld failure, OHE sag, signal interlock, tamping required, deep screening.
  • Ruled out — a nil report, a negated defect, or a TSR that has been lifted. Those sentences do not become jobs.
  • Ready to apply — defect type, running line, chainage, severity, days overdue, and whether a TSR is on. The line must exist at that chainage. A missing or ambiguous field stays in review.
  • Handoff — only ready records enter the open backlog. The Divisional Maintenance Officer receives the summary. Formula 1, the days-to-critical forecast, Formula 2, and CP-SAT run after that.
The local reader matches this project’s own phrases. The external spaCy note is not imported, so that adapter stays pending.
01 · FORMULA 1 · defects.py

Score every open job

Track (TMS), traction (TDMS) and signal (SMMS) records share one backlog. The console score runs from 0 to 100. The solver keeps the unclipped score, so two severe jobs do not tie at 100. A separate forecast estimates days until severity reaches 80. It does not change Formula 1. Mandatory work is tried first, then the higher score.

02 · FORMULA 2 · defects.py

Pack them into one block

Jobs on the same running line and the same 25 kV feeder are chained when the next defect is within 3 km. Work time is the longest job plus 15 minutes. Separate visits would have added each job’s own block time.

03 · CP-SAT · solver.py

Fit it to the timetable

The selected bundle is placed inside the next legal window: weeknight 00:00–04:00, daytime 11:30–14:30, or Sunday 00:00–07:00. The envelope covers the depot run, the work, the return run, and protection or earthing. A train on that line is clear before the block opens, or it enters only after the block closes.

04 · ROI · metrics.py

Count the track returned

Each job’s own block time, added up, is compared with the time inside the bundle. Chaos score Z is weighted train delay plus the urgency-weighted start. Both appear on the ribbon and in this panel.

Formula 1 · Priority urgencyPᵢ ∈ [0, 100]
$$P_i = 10\,s_i + 0.35\cdot\frac{o_i}{30}\cdot 100 + 15\,v_i + 3\,t_i$$
  • $s_i$ — safety. Severity ÷ 20, capped at 5.
  • $o_i$ — days overdue, capped at 30.
  • $v_i$ — 1 when a temporary speed restriction is already on, otherwise 0.
  • $t_i$ — distinct trains on this running line within 1 km, counted across the day. The count is divided by 6 and held between 1 and 10.
A severe fracture on a busy section, already under caution, rises above a routine tamp. That order is what the planner protects first.
Formula 2 · One possession, then its lengthBUNDLE + ENVELOPE
Same line, same feeder, next defect within 3.0 km.
$$T_{bundle}=\max(T_i)+15 \qquad W=\lceil 2\,t_{transit}+T_{bundle}+B\rceil$$
$$Gain=\left(1-\frac{\sum T_{bundle}}{\sum T_{alone}}\right)\times 100$$
  • $T_{alone}$ — each defect’s own block, added up.
  • $t_{transit}$ — slowest machine, one way: tie tamper from Karjat at 35 km/h, ballast cleaner from Kalyan at 30 km/h, tower wagon from Lonavala at 40 km/h.
  • $B$ — 15 min to isolate and earth both ends on a power block, 5 min of protection on a traffic-only block.
Gain is the share of possession time handed back to trains. The envelope W is the clock time the solver must keep clear.
How CP-SAT searchesMINIMISE Z
The selected bundle gets a start inside its legal window. The end is fixed once the envelope is known. Each train may take a delay only up to its patience.
$$\min Z=\sum_{trains} W_{type}\cdot delay+\sum_{blocks}\max\!\left(1,\operatorname{round}(W_{post}\cdot urgency)\right)\cdot start$$
  • $W_{type}=300$ for premium, superfast, express and local. $2$ for freight and bankers.
  • $W_{post}$ starts at 50 on this screen. It is multiplied by that bundle’s Formula 1 urgency, so a later start costs more on a more urgent job.
Three weight profiles are solved in the next legal windows that can hold this bundle. Punctuality-first keeps 300 / 2 / 50. Asset-first divides the passenger weight by 4, with a floor of 20, and triples $W_{post}$. Mega-weights divides the passenger weight by 2, with a floor of 40, sets the freight weight to 1, and doubles $W_{post}$. The profile does not change the length of the window. Every train stays in the safety check.
Rules the search cannot breakHARD CONSTRAINTS
1
No crash. The train is clear before the block opens, or it enters only after the block closes. The check is on the closed running line.
2
Premium and superfast stay on time. Their patience is 0 minutes, so Vande Bharat and Deccan Queen class paths keep the working timetable. Express and local may wait at most 15 minutes. Freight and bankers may wait at most 120.
3
Division machines are a pool. Two tie tampers, one ballast cleaner, two tower wagons. A type cannot be booked past that fleet.
4
25 kV isolation is line-specific. A power block isolates that feeder’s line and earths both ends before work. The other lines keep power and stay open.
5
The possession has a fixed length. W must fit inside the legal window, or that window is not used. OPTIMAL means this Z is the best. FEASIBLE means a safe plan is in hand inside the time limit.
